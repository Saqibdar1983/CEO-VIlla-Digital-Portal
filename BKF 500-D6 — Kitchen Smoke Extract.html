Troubleshooting Checklist

Symptoms • Causes • Corrective Actions
SymptomNo.Possible CauseCorrective Action
Fan not operating / does not start1No power supply, blown fuse, tripped breaker, or isolator switched OFF.Check incoming supply, isolator, fuse, and breaker. Restore correct power supply only after confirming safe condition.
2Loose, disconnected, or incorrect electrical wiring.Isolate power, inspect terminals, tighten connections, and verify wiring as per approved wiring diagram.
3Motor protection, PTC, thermal overload, or VFD fault is active.Check protection relay/VFD fault code, reset only after finding the cause. Verify motor current against nameplate.
4Motor defective or winding damaged.Test insulation resistance and winding continuity. Repair or replace motor as per Systemair/motor manufacturer recommendation.
5Impeller blocked by foreign object or mechanical obstruction.Isolate power, open access safely, remove obstruction, clean fan, and manually check free rotation before restarting.
Wrong rotation / airflow opposite direction1Three-phase supply phase sequence is incorrect.Check rotation direction after start-up. Interchange two phases at supply side if rotation is wrong.
2Incorrect VFD output phase connection.Verify VFD output wiring and rotation command. Correct phase sequence and retest.
3Fan installed opposite to intended airflow direction.Check arrow mark on casing and approved duct airflow direction. Correct installation if fan orientation is wrong.
Insufficient airflow1Duct system blocked, damper closed, fire/smoke damper closed, or external static pressure higher than design.Inspect duct route, dampers, louvers, and smoke/fire damper status. Remove blockage and set dampers to approved position.
2Impeller dirty, grease/dust deposits, or fan casing fouled.Clean impeller and casing. For kitchen/grease duty, increase cleaning frequency and check filters/grease traps.
3Fan rotating in wrong direction.Check actual wheel rotation and correct phase sequence/VFD wiring.
4VFD frequency set too low or speed control command incorrect.Check operating frequency and control signal. Adjust within approved range and fan nameplate limits.
5Flexible connector collapsed, duct leakage, or access door left open.Inspect ductwork, flexible connections, and access doors. Seal leakage and repair damaged sections.
6Incorrect fan selection or operating point outside approved performance curve.Compare measured airflow/static pressure with approved selection. Escalate to design/supplier if fan cannot meet duty.
Excessive noise1Non-typical bearing noise or defective motor bearing.Stop fan if noise is abnormal. Check bearing condition and replace motor/bearing through authorized service.
2Impeller unbalanced due to dirt, grease, or damage.Clean impeller, check for damage, and balance/replace impeller if vibration remains.
3Loose mounting bolts, loose casing, or fan not securely anchored.Tighten anchor bolts, frame, casing screws, and supports. Verify anti-vibration mounts if provided.
4High air velocity, duct turbulence, or pressure pulsation.Check duct transitions, bends, dampers, and system resistance. Rectify turbulence and confirm operation within fan curve.
5VFD/motor electrical noise due to missing sine/dU/dt filter or long cable.Check VFD installation. Use approved all-pole sine filter or dU/dt filter where required by Systemair/motor instructions.
Excessive vibration1Impeller imbalance caused by dirt build-up or mechanical damage.Clean impeller and inspect blades. Balance or replace impeller if vibration exceeds acceptable level.
2Fan base, frame, or duct connection not rigid / not aligned.Check supports, base frame, flexible connector, and duct alignment. Re-level and secure installation.
3Fan operating near unstable point or pressure pulsation due to system condition.Check airflow, static pressure, damper position, and duct restrictions. Adjust system to stable operating point.
4Motor bearing or shaft problem.Check bearing noise, shaft runout, and motor condition. Replace defective motor or bearing assembly.
Motor overheating / high current1Overcurrent due to fan operating outside nameplate limits or excessive airflow load.Measure voltage, current, frequency, and static pressure. Adjust operating point within nameplate and approved selection.
2Overvoltage, undervoltage, phase imbalance, or single phasing.Check supply voltage and phase balance. Correct electrical supply issue before restarting.
3Blocked cooling path, dirty motor, or poor ventilation around external motor.Clean motor cooling fins and ensure well-ventilated installation area. Remove any obstruction around motor.
4Excessive switching frequency or frequent start/stop operation.Review control sequence. BKF is intended for continuous operation; avoid extreme switching.
5VFD parameters incorrect or frequency outside approved operating range.Check VFD settings, acceleration/deceleration, min/max frequency, motor data, and protection settings.
6Impeller rubbing or mechanical resistance.Isolate power, rotate impeller manually, check clearances, remove obstruction, and correct alignment.
VFD fault / drive trip1Incorrect VFD parameter settings for motor rated current, voltage, frequency, or ramp time.Enter motor nameplate data correctly and adjust ramp/protection settings as per approved commissioning procedure.
2Voltage peaks at motor terminals due to long cable or missing output filter.Install/verify sine filter or dU/dt filter as recommended. Check cable length and earthing.
3Fan load too high because system resistance/damper condition is incorrect.Check damper status, duct pressure, and airflow. Remove restrictions and restart after confirming load is normal.
4Overtemperature or overcurrent alarm generated by motor protection.Investigate motor temperature/current cause. Do not repeatedly reset without correction.
Pressure pulsation / unstable operation1Fan operating outside stable area of performance curve.Measure airflow/static pressure and compare with selection. Adjust dampers or system resistance to approved operating point.
2Duct design issue: sharp bend, poor transition, or obstruction near fan inlet/outlet.Inspect duct geometry around fan. Provide proper straight length/transitions or rectify obstruction where possible.
3Damper too close to fan or partially closed causing turbulence.Adjust damper position and review location. Keep operation within stable range.
Smoke extraction / emergency mode not functioning1Motor protective devices, PTC, or VFD not correctly bypassed for fire mode where required by approved sequence.Verify fire-mode control logic with approved fire strategy. Ensure required bypass/max-speed operation is functional.
2Fan does not switch to maximum speed after fire signal or after short supply interruption.Test fire signal, control relay, VFD bypass/contactors, and restart logic. Correct sequence before acceptance.
3Incorrect temperature/time class or fan identification for smoke duty.Check nameplate, approval submittal, and fan certification. Do not use fan for smoke extraction if rating is not compliant.
4Fan installed inside wrong fire zone or not in well-ventilated approved location.Review installation location against approved drawings and Systemair instructions. Relocate or protect as required.
Foreign object / impeller contact1Foreign particles entered fan due to missing protection or open duct during construction.Remove foreign object, inspect impeller/casing damage, clean internal area, and install protection until final operation.
2Outlet/inlet not protected where access to impeller is possible.Install approved protective grid/accessory where required and confirm no person can touch rotating parts during operation.
3Impeller shifted, casing damaged, or clearance lost due to handling/installation impact.Check casing alignment and impeller clearance. Repair casing or replace damaged components.
Grease/dust deposit build-up1Kitchen exhaust or dusty air without adequate filtering/cleaning frequency.Clean fan and ductwork. Confirm application is suitable and improve filtration/grease management where required.
2Maintenance access not provided or access door not used for periodic cleaning.Provide safe access, establish cleaning schedule, and record maintenance activity.
3Fan used for medium with excessive dust/grease beyond intended limits.Review application suitability. Provide appropriate filtration or consult supplier for correct fan/application.
