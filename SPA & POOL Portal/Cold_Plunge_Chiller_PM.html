11Confirm cold plunge pool is receiving chilled water return and water level is normal
C.  ELECTRICAL CHECK

A.  VISUAL INSPECTION
1Inspect heat pump housing and casing for cracks, corrosion, paint damage, or impact marks — clean housing with damp cloth only (the manual prohibits detergents or household products as these degrade casing surface)
2Inspect all water pipe connections (Dn63 inlet and outlet) for leaks, scale deposits, or corrosion at fittings and joints
3Inspect By-Pass valve assembly — confirm all 3 valves move freely and are positioned correctly; check valve bodies and pipe connections for leaks
4Inspect condensate drain pipe — confirm unobstructed and draining freely; flush with clean water if slow draining
5Inspect fan guard / protection grid — confirm intact, securely fixed, and no corrosion; confirm minimum clearances around unit are maintained for adequate airflow
6Confirm unit is not exposed to snow (if applicable, snow guard must be installed at least 2m above unit per manual)
B.  EVAPORATOR INSPECTION & CLEANING
7Inspect evaporator fins at rear of unit for dust accumulation, debris, leaves, or biological growth blocking airflow
8Isolate power at main isolator before cleaning evaporator
9Clean evaporator fins carefully using soft brush vacuum cleaner or CleanPac-type cleaner as specified by manufacturer — do not use high-pressure water jet directly on fins; do not use acid-based or corrosive cleaners
10After cleaning, confirm fins are free of debris and air can pass through evaporator freely
11Inspect fan blades for debris accumulation, damage, or deformation — do not touch fan while running; only inspect with power isolated
12Restore power after evaporator area is dry; confirm unit restarts normally
C.  REFRIGERATION CIRCUIT VISUAL CHECK
13Visually inspect all accessible refrigerant pipe connections, joints, and components for oil stains (oil stain indicates refrigerant R410A leak)
14Check pressure gauge — confirm reading is above 80 PSI; reading below 80 PSI indicates possible refrigerant leak; do not operate unit if refrigerant is low and report immediately for investigation by certified engineer
15Check for abnormal frost or ice formation on refrigerant pipes (frost in unexpected locations indicates refrigerant undercharge or flow restriction)
16NOTE: Refrigerant circuit (R410A) work must only be performed by a certified refrigeration engineer; do not open refrigerant circuit or adjust pressure without certification
D.  ELECTRICAL CHECKS (VISUAL)
17Confirm no fault/error codes on wired remote controller display — press Status then Fault Query to review fault history
18Inspect external power supply cable from distribution board to unit for damage, abrasion, UV degradation, or heat marks
19Confirm electrical panel door on unit is securely closed and sealed against moisture ingress (unit is IPX4 rated)
20Confirm earth connection at power supply terminal block is intact and not corroded (mandatory per manual — 400V 3-phase supply)
E.  WATER QUALITY CHECK
21Test cold plunge water pH — pH must be maintained between 6.9 and 8.0 per manufacturer requirement (pH outside this range will damage titanium heat exchanger); record: pH _______
22Confirm chemical treatment system (Cl / pH dosing) is installed and operating downstream (after heat pump) in the hydraulic circuit — treatment upstream of heat pump is prohibited
23Check water clarity — no unusual cloudiness, discoloration, or scale particles indicating chemical imbalance
F.  PERFORMANCE CHECK
24Record: Water temperature (display): _______ °C | Setpoint: _______ °C | Ambient air temperature: _______ °C
25Confirm unit is achieving and maintaining setpoint — if unit runs continuously without reaching setpoint, investigate water flow rate (rated 30 m³/h) and refrigerant charge
26Record current draw with clamp meter if available: _______ A (rated 27.0A at 380V/3N/60Hz; significant deviation indicates fault)
27Confirm EER is acceptable — any notable reduction in cooling output with same power input indicates fouled evaporator, refrigerant issue, or heat exchanger scaling

A.  VISUAL & SAFETY INSPECTION
1Inspect heat pump housing, casing, mounting feet, and base frame for corrosion, cracks, or structural damage — unit weighs 420 kg; confirm base is stable and all mounting points are secure
2Inspect all water pipe connections (Dn63) for leaks, corrosion, or scale buildup — tighten or replace fittings as required
3Inspect By-Pass valve assembly — test all 3 valves for free and full operation; inspect valve bodies, seals, and pipe connections for leaks
4Inspect condensate drain pipe — confirm unobstructed; clean drain if scale or biological growth present
5Inspect fan guard / protection grid for corrosion or damage — confirm guard is in place and secured (mandatory per manual if unit is in a non-restricted access area)
6Confirm no objects, vegetation, or construction materials are within the required clearance distance around the unit
B.  EVAPORATOR FULL SERVICE CLEAN
7Isolate power at main isolator before starting evaporator service
8Clean evaporator fins thoroughly using CleanPac-type cleaner as specified by manufacturer — apply with soft brush, allow to act per product instructions, then rinse with gentle low-pressure water from top to bottom of fins
9Inspect fins for bent or mechanically damaged sections — straighten bent fins with fin comb if required; do not use excessive force
10Confirm evaporator is fully free of debris, dust, scale, and biological growth after service
11Restore power after evaporator area is completely dry — confirm unit restarts in Inverter Cooling mode without fault codes
C.  REFRIGERANT & REFRIGERATION CIRCUIT — FULL SERVICE (CERTIFIED ENGINEER)
12Carry out annual refrigerant leak check using approved electronic leak detector on all joints, connections, compressor, and heat exchanger — record result: PASS / FAIL and any leak locations found
13Check pressure gauge condition — confirm gauge is undamaged and reading is above 80 PSI (reading below 80 PSI confirms refrigerant loss; investigate and recharge only with R410A by certified engineer)
14Inspect all refrigerant pipes, fittings, joints, and compressors for oil stains or frost indicating active refrigerant leak
15Check high-pressure switch and low-pressure switch — confirm both are correctly connected to refrigeration circuit and will electrically interrupt compressor on pressure trip (test if test facility is available)
16Inspect around compressors and refrigerant components for traces of corrosion — any corrosion on refrigerant components must be reported and assessed by certified engineer
17NOTE: Refrigerant R410A is a high-pressure refrigerant (max operating pressure 4.4 MPa); only a certified refrigeration engineer may work on the refrigerant circuit; do not open refrigerant circuit without certification
D.  ELECTRICAL INSPECTION — FULL SERVICE
18Isolate power at main isolator; open electrical panel on unit using screwdriver as per manual procedure
19Inspect all power cables at terminal block (Phase A, Phase B, Phase C, Neutral, Earth) for insulation damage, heat marks, corrosion, or loose terminations
20Check and firmly tighten all terminal block connections — particularly Phase A, B, C, Neutral, and Earth terminals (unit operates at 400V 3-phase; loose connections create arc and fire risk)
21Test earth (ground) connection continuity — confirm earth at power supply terminal block is intact; check chassis earth connection (mandatory per manual)
22Inspect pump control terminals (T1, T2) for connection to circulation pump relay — confirm wiring is secure and correctly connected
23Inspect capacitors (if accessible) for signs of swelling, bulging, or electrolyte leakage
24Close electrical panel securely; confirm sealed against moisture (IPX4 protection must be maintained)
E.  WATER CIRCUIT & HEAT EXCHANGER CHECK
25Test By-Pass valve operation manually — confirm all 3 valves operate freely through full range and hold position correctly
26Check water pressure on water side — maximum operating pressure is 0.55 MPa; confirm system pressure is within this limit
27Check water pressure differential across heat exchanger — rated pressure loss is 40 kPa at 30 m³/h; significantly higher pressure loss indicates scaling inside titanium heat exchanger
28If scaling is confirmed in heat exchanger, arrange de-scaling service by qualified technician using manufacturer-approved de-scaling procedure; do not use acid de-scaling without checking titanium heat exchanger compatibility
29Test cold plunge water pH: _______ (maintain strictly between 6.9 and 8.0 per manual — pH out of this range causes irreversible heat exchanger damage)
30Confirm water flow rate — rated 30 m³/h at Dn63; significant reduction indicates blockage, scaled heat exchanger, or pump fault
F.  PERFORMANCE COMMISSIONING CHECK
31Restore power; confirm unit starts in Inverter Cooling mode without fault codes or Protection symbol
32Verify wired remote controller clock and time program settings are correct
33Record: Water temp in: _______ °C | Water temp out: _______ °C | Ambient air: _______ °C | Setpoint: _______ °C
34Record current draw: _______ A (rated 27.0A at 380V/3N/60Hz; rated power 15.0kW); deviation above rated indicates fault
35Monitor through full cooling cycle — confirm unit reaches setpoint, cycles correctly, and no fault codes appear
36Confirm noise level is normal — unit rated below 62 dB(A) at 1m; abnormal noise after service must be investigated before returning to service
G.  DOCUMENTATION
37Record all maintenance activities, findings, and corrective actions in equipment history log
38Record refrigerant leak test result, leak test method used, and any corrective action taken (refrigerant R410A quantity added if applicable)
39List all parts replaced or services performed with description
40Update next 3-monthly and 6-monthly service dates in maintenance planner
