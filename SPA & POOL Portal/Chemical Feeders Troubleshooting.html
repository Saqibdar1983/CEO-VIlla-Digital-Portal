SPA & POOL FACILITYChemical Feeders Troubleshooting
ProblemProbable CauseRemedy
LOSS OF CHEMICAL RESIDUAL1. Pump setting too low1. Adjust to higher setting (feeder must be operating during the stroke length adjustment).
LOSS OF CHEMICAL RESIDUAL2. Scale at injection point2. Clean injection parts with 8% muriatic acid or undiluted vinegar. See Maintenance Section.
LOSS OF CHEMICAL RESIDUAL3. Solution container allowed to run dry3. Refill the tank with solution and prime. See Start-Up Section.
TOO MUCH CHEMICAL1. Feeder setting too high1. Lower feeder setting (feeder must be operating to adjust the dial).
TOO MUCH CHEMICAL2. Chemical in solution tank too rich2. Dilute chemical solution. NOTE: For chemical that reacts with water, it may be necessary to purchase a more dilute grade of chemical direct from chemical supplier.
TOO MUCH CHEMICAL3. Siphoning of chemical into well or main line3. Test for suction or vacuum at the injection point. If suction exists, install an anti-siphon valve. See Figure G.
LEAKAGE AROUND TUBING CONNECTIONS1. Worn tube ends1. Cut off end of tubing (about 1") and then slip on as before or replace suction valve housing and compression fitting to prevent leakage.
LEAKAGE AROUND TUBING CONNECTIONS2. Chemical attack2. Consult your chemical supplier for compatible materials.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED1. Leak in suction side of pump1. Examine suction tubing. If worn at the end, cut approximately an inch off and replace or replace valve body and coupling nut.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED2. Valve seats not sealing2. Clean valve seats if dirty or replace with proper material if deterioration is noted.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED3. Low setting on feeder3. When pumping against pressure, the dial should be set above 40% maximum rated capacity for a reliable feed rate.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED4. Low solution level4. Solution must be above foot valve.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED5. Diaphragm ruptured5. Replace diaphragm as shown in "Service" Section. Check for pressure above 100 PSI (7 BAR) at the injection point. NOTE: Chemical incompatibility with diaphragm material can cause diaphragm rupture and leakage around the pump head.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED6. Pump head cracked or broken6. Replace pump head as shown in "Service" Section. Do not use pipe tape or other sealants. Make sure fittings hand tight only. Using pliers or wrench can crack pump head. Also, chemical incompatibility can cause cracking and subsequent leakage.
FAILURE TO PUMP OR FEED7. Pumphead contains air or chlorine gas7. While pump is running, turn bleed valve adjustment screw counter-clockwise until air is purged. Close bleed valve.
UNIT LOSES PRIME1. Dirty check valve1. Remove and replace or clean off any scale or sediment.
UNIT LOSES PRIME2. Ball checks not seating or not sealing properly2. Check seat and ball checks for chips, clean gently. If deformity or deterioration is noted, replace part with proper material. Chemical crystallization can hold check valves open, therefore the valves must be disassembled and cleaned. Be sure to replace all parts as shown in the Parts Diagram (at the end of the manual).
UNIT LOSES PRIME3. Solution container allowed to run dry3. Refill the tank with solution and prime.
LEAKAGE AT FITTING1. Loose fittings1. All fittings can be hand tightened to prevent leakage. Clean off chemicals which have spilled on feeder.
LEAKAGE AT FITTING2. Broken or twisted gasket2. Check gaskets and replace if broken or damaged.
LEAKAGE AT FITTING3. Chemical attack3. Consult your chemical supplier for compatible materials.
FEEDER WILL NOT PRIME1. Too much pressure at discharge1. Open bleed valve and circulate fluid until all air is purged from pump head assembly. Close bleed valve.
FEEDER WILL NOT PRIME2. Check valves not sealing2. Disassemble, loosen, clean and check for deterioration or swelling. Reassemble and wet the valve assembly, then prime. See INSTALLATION Section.
FEEDER WILL NOT PRIME3. Output dials not set at maximum3. Always prime pump with output dial set at maximum rated capacity.
ANTI-SIPHON VALVE MALFUNCTION1. Scale or particles have plugged diaphragm4. Remove, clean and reassemble, being careful not to wrinkle the diaphragm. Check sequence and position of parts to be sure reassembly is correct.
ANTI-SIPHON VALVE MALFUNCTION2. Ruptured valves5. Consult your distributor for replacement.
PUMP MOTOR STALLS1. Pumping against excessive pressure1. Test pressure to determine if it exceeds feeder specifications. If so, consult your distributor.
PUMP MOTOR STALLS2. Low voltage to feeder2. Make sure voltage of power source matches the voltage on the feeder specifications label. If not transformers are available.
MOTOR RUNNING VERY HOT1. Low voltage1. Power supply voltage should match voltage on feeder specification label.
MOTOR RUNNING VERY HOT2. If using a stepdown transformer, it may be undersized for the feeder2. Check the transformer to be sure it has at least 100 watts capacity.
