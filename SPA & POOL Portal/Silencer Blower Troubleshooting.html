AIR SUPPLY SILENCER: TROUBLESHOOTING
SymptomProbable CauseCorrective Action
Blower hums but won't start• Seized motor impeller.
• Volts dropped below 208V under load.		• Turn off power, check if the impeller rotates freely with a screwdriver via the bottom intake.
• Check supply voltage at the terminal block while trying to start the unit.
Blower runs for 5–10 mins, then cuts out• Restricted airflow causing thermal trip.
• Pipe diameter is reduced.
• Too many 90° elbows.		• Verify the line is 2" minimum all the way to the pool/spa jets.
• Check the air injectors/jets for scale or blockages.
• Allow 30 minutes for the internal thermal overload to reset automatically.
Trips the GFCI breaker instantly• Water has bypassed the Hartford loop/check valve into the motor.
• Shorted winding.		• Disconnect power completely. Remove the blower and check for moisture inside the housing.
• If wet, dry thoroughly before re-testing. Replace the check valve immediately.
Blower runs, but no bubbles in the spa• Stuck check valve.
• Water level is too high.
• Air line has a major crack underground.		• Inspect the inline 1/4 lb spring check valve; ensure it isn't stuck closed or installed backward.
• Verify the air line is clear of debris or blockages.
Excessive noise or high-pitched screeching• Worn motor bearings.
• Blower body is vibrating against walls/pad.		• If the screech is internal, the bearings are failing (usually due to past moisture exposure). The motor assembly must be replaced.
• Ensure the base is resting on an anti-vibration rubber pad.
DEAD MOTOR CHECK SEQUENCE
StepDiagnostic ObjectiveAction & Field Verification Procedure
Step 1Verify Power SupplyUse your digital multimeter to verify you have 220–240V AC across the two hot lines at the control panel/timer relay. If there is no voltage, check the breaker, GFCI, or automation panel programming.
Step 2Check Thermal Overload StateIf the blower was running recently, the internal thermal protector may have tripped (triggers at ~105°C / 221°F). Wait a full 30 to 45 minutes for the casing to cool down completely, then try starting it again.
Step 3Isolate the Blower PlumbingUnscrew the sheet metal screw and back the blower off the 2" PVC stub. Turn it on briefly while completely disconnected from the pipe.

• If it runs fine unattached: Your issue is high backpressure in the underground lines (blocked jets or water-logged line).
• If it still won't turn on: The internal motor windings or internal wiring harness are damaged.
