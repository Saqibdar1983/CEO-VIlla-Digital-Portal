A.  VISUAL INSPECTION
1Inspect sauna heater body, outer casing, and stone guard frame for dents, rust, corrosion, discolouration, or burn marks — discolouration on casing may indicate overheating
2Check that the heater is securely fixed to wall or floor — inspect all mounting brackets and fixing bolts for tightness and corrosion; tighten any loose fasteners
3Confirm the stone guard / wire basket is intact and properly seated — guard must fully enclose the stone/element area to prevent accidental contact
4Check that no foreign objects (towels, debris, cleaning cloths) are on or inside the heater stone basket
5Confirm required clearances are maintained: minimum 500 mm clearance above the heater, minimum 200 mm to side walls — no combustible materials within clearance zone
B.  SAUNA STONE INSPECTION
6Inspect sauna stones — confirm stones are correctly arranged with clear air gaps to allow airflow around heating elements; no stone should rest directly on a heating element
7Examine each visible stone for cracks, crumbling, or heavy fragmentation — crumbled or deteriorated stones block airflow and cause element overheating; remove and replace any badly fragmented stones immediately
8Check for accumulation of stone dust or fine debris at the base of the stone basket — excessive powder indicates stone deterioration; note condition and plan stone replacement if significant debris is present
9Rearrange stones as necessary to restore correct stacking: larger stones at the base surrounding elements with clear air gaps; smaller/medium stones layered on top; no stone bridging across elements or blocking air channels
C.  ELECTRICAL CHECK
10SAFETY: Switch off the heater at the controller and isolate power at the local MCB/isolator before any electrical inspection; confirm de-energised with a voltage tester
11Inspect external power supply cable entry at the heater for damage, chafing, kinking, or abrasion; confirm cable gland is tight and sealing correctly
12Inspect all accessible wiring at conduit/cable entries for signs of heat damage, discolouration, or exposed conductors — do not open internal terminal covers during monthly inspection; report any concerns for specialist attention
13Confirm all conduit entries, cable glands, and access panel covers are correctly closed and sealed — heater is rated IPX4 (splash-proof); any open entry compromises water ingress protection
D.  HIGH-TEMPERATURE CUT-OFF (TCO) STATUS CHECK
14With power restored, confirm the high-temperature thermal cut-off (TCO) has not tripped — if heater fails to energise after correct controller operation, check TCO reset button per manufacturer instructions
15If TCO has tripped since last inspection: investigate root cause before resetting (check stone arrangement, element condition, and airflow) — do NOT reset TCO without identifying and correcting the cause of overheating
E.  CONTROLLER & OPERATIONAL CHECK
16Restore power and activate heater at the controller — confirm display illuminates and shows current sauna room temperature reading
17Set operating temperature (80–90 °C as per venue schedule) and confirm the heater calls for heat — heating indicator on the controller display should show heating mode
18Verify all controller LEDs, digital display segments, and indicator lights are functioning correctly — report any faulty indicators
19Confirm sauna room temperature rises toward setpoint — ZL16.2 (16.2 kW) should reach 80–90 °C within approximately 25–35 minutes from cold start
20Confirm timer function operates correctly and heater shuts down automatically at the end of the set period
F.  DOCUMENTATION
21Record all inspection findings, stone condition noted, and any corrective actions taken in the equipment maintenance log
22Update maintenance schedule with today's inspection date and next monthly due date

A.  SAFETY ISOLATION
1Switch off heater at the controller; isolate power at the electrical distribution panel (MCB/circuit breaker); apply lockout/tagout as required by site safety procedure; allow heater to cool for a minimum of 2 hours after last operation before proceeding
2Confirm power is isolated using a calibrated voltage tester at the heater terminal block before opening any covers or removing stones
B.  SAUNA STONES — FULL ANNUAL REPLACEMENT
3Remove ALL sauna stones from the heater basket — full stone replacement is mandatory for commercial installations annually; deteriorated stones are the primary cause of heating element failure
4Inspect each removed stone individually — discard all crumbled, fragmented, severely cracked, or heavily deteriorated stones; do not reuse any stone that is structurally compromised
5Clean stone basket interior — remove all stone dust, small debris, and residue; vacuum or wipe clean; heating elements must be clearly visible and accessible after cleaning
6Inspect stone basket / wire guard frame for deformation, rust, or corrosion — repair or replace basket if structurally compromised
7Install fresh approved sauna stones: olivine, diabase, or equivalent heat-resistant volcanic rock; stone diameter 4–7 cm; do NOT use limestone, sandstone, marble, or river pebbles — these fracture explosively under heat
8Stack stones correctly: LARGER stones at base surrounding elements with clear air gaps; SMALLER/MEDIUM stones layered on top; no stone to rest directly on any heating element surface; visible air channels must be maintained throughout
C.  HEATING ELEMENT FULL INSPECTION
9With power isolated and stones removed, inspect each heating element for: discolouration (dark brown/black = overheating), physical deformation, bends, cracks, or visible damage to element sheath
10Inspect element mounting brackets, support rods, and flanges for corrosion, rust, or mechanical damage — corroded or loose mounts must be repaired before reinstalling stones
11Test element resistance with a calibrated digital multimeter — ZL16.2 (16.2 kW, 400 V, 3-phase): expected resistance approximately 9–10 Ω per phase circuit; significant deviation indicates element degradation. Measured: L1-L2 _____ Ω  |  L2-L3 _____ Ω  |  L1-L3 _____ Ω
12Perform insulation resistance (IR) test using a 500 V DC megohmmeter — minimum acceptable IR is 1 MΩ; reading below 1 MΩ indicates moisture ingress or element failure requiring replacement. Measured: L1-PE _____ MΩ  |  L2-PE _____ MΩ  |  L3-PE _____ MΩ
13If any element is defective: replace ONLY with manufacturer-approved elements rated for ZL16.2 (16.2 kW, 400 VAC, 3N, 50/60 Hz, IPX4) — contact OCS/ZL Series authorised supplier for genuine replacement parts
D.  ELECTRICAL WIRING FULL INSPECTION
14Open heater terminal compartment with power isolated and zero-voltage confirmed — inspect all supply terminals (L1, L2, L3, N, PE) for loose connections, discolouration from heat, corrosion, oxidation, or burn marks
15Tighten all terminal screws to the torque specified in the IOM manual — loose connections at 400 V / 3-phase are a fire and arc-flash hazard; document any connections requiring re-termination
16Inspect supply cable from terminal block to distribution board — check cable insulation along the full accessible length for cracking, chafing, heat damage, or rodent damage
17Inspect the earth (PE) protective bonding conductor — confirm secure connection at heater body bonding lug, at terminal block PE terminal, and at distribution panel earth bar; earth continuity is a life safety requirement
18Inspect temperature sensor wiring, thermostat wiring, and control signal cables — confirm all connections are tight and insulation is undamaged
19Inspect high-temperature cut-off (TCO) device — confirm TCO is correctly positioned adjacent to the heating element zone; check TCO wiring and connections for security and correct routing
E.  HIGH-TEMPERATURE CUT-OFF (TCO) — FUNCTIONAL TEST
20Confirm TCO is in the non-tripped (reset) state before restoring power
21CAUTION — TCO functional test must be carried out by a qualified electrician only: allow heater to reach elevated temperature while monitoring with an independent thermometer; confirm TCO trips at rated cut-off temperature. TCO trip temperature recorded: _____ °C
22Confirm TCO resets correctly after cooling; restore power and confirm unit returns to normal heating operation after TCO test
F.  CONTROLLER & SENSOR CALIBRATION CHECK
23Restore power; activate controller — confirm display illuminates and shows room temperature reading; confirm no fault codes or error messages
24Compare controller temperature display with an independent calibrated reference thermometer at sensor height in sauna room — drift of more than ±3 °C should be investigated and corrected. Controller: _____ °C  |  Reference: _____ °C  |  Deviation: _____ °C
25Confirm timer function operates correctly over a full set/countdown/automatic shutdown cycle
26Inspect controller wiring connections inside control panel — confirm all terminals are tight and cable insulation is undamaged
27Confirm all controller LEDs, display segments, and indicator lights are functioning correctly
G.  IPX4 PROTECTION INTEGRITY CHECK
28Confirm all cable gland entries are correctly tightened and sealed after servicing — IPX4 protection (splash-proof from any direction) requires all entries to be sealed
29Confirm all terminal cover gaskets and access panel seals are in good condition and correctly reinstalled — replace any damaged or compressed gaskets
30Confirm all fixing screws, stone guard brackets, and enclosure covers are reinstalled correctly and all fasteners are fully tightened after inspection
H.  PERFORMANCE COMMISSIONING
31Restore power; activate heater — confirm unit calls for heat and no fault codes appear on display
32Monitor initial heat-up cycle with new stones — ZL16.2 should reach 80–90 °C within approximately 25–35 minutes from cold start; minor stone crackling is normal during first heat cycle with new stones. Heat-up start: _____  |  Temp at 30 min: _____ °C  |  Time to 80 °C: _____
33Monitor for any electrical burning smell during heat-up — if detected, IMMEDIATELY isolate power and investigate before continuing commissioning; do not proceed with any burning smell present
34Confirm heater maintains set temperature at steady state — no erratic cycling or temperature undershoot below setpoint by more than 5 °C
35Confirm automatic shutdown at end of programmed timer period — heater de-energises completely at set time
I.  DOCUMENTATION
36Record all inspection findings and measurement data (element resistance, IR test results, TCO trip temperature, controller calibration deviation) in the equipment maintenance history log
37Record all parts replaced: sauna stones (quantity and specification), elements, or other components — include specifications, supplier, and invoice/delivery reference
