1.  PURPOSE & SCOPE
1.1  Purpose

This Method Statement defines the approved procedure for the supply, inspection, installation, filter media loading, piping connection, and commissioning of all sand filters and their associated 6-way multiport valves within the SPA & Pool Facility of the CEO Villa Project.

The document ensures that all works are executed in full compliance with the manufacturer's installation manuals, approved shop drawing SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1, relevant international standards, and project quality requirements.

1.2  Scope of Work

This method statement covers the following three (3) filter units and their associated multiport valves:

SF-CPC-03Cold Plunge Sand FilterPentair Tagelus TA-4040 GPM50 PSI (3.4 bar)Pentair Hi-Flow 1.5" 6-Way

The scope includes: unpacking and inspection of filter tanks, gravel and sand loading per manufacturer specification, installation of 6-way multiport valves, piping connections including isolation valves and pressure gauges, initial backwash and rinse cycle, functional testing, and handover documentation.

5.  HEALTH, SAFETY & ENVIRONMENT
5.1  Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

All personnel on site must wear at minimum: Safety helmet, safety shoes (steel toe), hi-visibility vest, safety gloves, and safety glasses. When handling dry sand or gravel, use a dust mask (minimum FFP2) and safety goggles. When applying PVC solvent cement or primer, use chemical-resistant gloves and ensure adequate ventilation.

5.2  Manual Handling
WARNING:The Pentair TR140 filter tank (SF-ODP-01) weighs approximately 52 kg (115 lbs) when empty. Always use minimum two (2) persons for lifting. Do not attempt to lift without team coordination. Use a sack trolley for moving filter tanks across the pump room.

Each bag of filter sand weighs 40 kg. Use mechanical handling (sack trolley) to transport from storage to pump room. Two persons required to lift and pour sand bags. Bend knees when lifting – never bend the back. Use proper grip on bags.

5.3  Work Area Safety
  • Barricade the pump room during filter media loading – fine sand dust is a respiratory hazard.
  • Keep the work area clean and dry at all times. Sand spilled on floor is a slip hazard.
  • Isolate electrical supply to pump motors before making any piping connections.
  • Do not apply pressure test without ensuring all personnel are clear of the filter area.
  • Keep a fire extinguisher accessible when using PVC solvent cement (flammable).
5.4  Chemical Safety (PVC Solvent Cement)

PVC solvent cement and primer are flammable and emit harmful vapors. Ensure ventilation is adequate. Do not smoke or use open flame in the area during application. Store cement away from heat sources. Dispose of empty containers as chemical waste per project environmental plan.

5.5  Housekeeping

Remove all packaging material, empty sand bags, and waste after each work stage. Sand on the pump room floor must be swept up immediately to prevent slip hazards and contamination of drainage. Filter room to be left clean and tidy at the end of each working day.

6.  INSTALLATION PROCEDURE
CRITICAL WARNING:STOP PUMP BEFORE CHANGING VALVE POSITION AT ANY TIME. Rotating the 6-way multiport valve handle while the pump is running will destroy the internal spider gasket and void the valve warranty. This is the most common and most expensive mistake in sand filter installation.
6.1  Pre-Installation Activities
6.1.1  Material Inspection
  1. Receive and inspect all filter tanks, multiport valves, and filter media bags against the approved MIR (Material Inspection Request) and the Delivery Order from Bin Talib Super.
  2. Check each filter tank nameplate against the Asset Register: Model (TR140 / TA-40), Part Number (140222 / 140219), and Serial Number. Record findings on the MIR.
  3. Inspect filter tanks for cracks, dents, or damage to the tank body, clamp ring, or top opening. Reject any damaged units.
  4. Open each filter and inspect the internal laterals assembly. All laterals must be fully seated in the hub at the base of the standpipe. Count all laterals – TR140 has 8 laterals; TA-40 has 6. Report any broken laterals before proceeding.
  5. Inspect multiport valves (SFV-ODP-01, SFV-INP-02, SFV-CPC-03). Check the valve body for cracks. Rotate the handle through all 6 positions to verify smooth operation and positive detent click at each position.
  6. Check the date on filter sand bags. Sand must be dry and free of lumps. If bags have been wet or stored without cover, reject and request replacement.
  7. Count and record all bags received: 4 bags × 40 kg silica sand (SF-ODP-01); 3 bags × 40 kg gravel (SF-ODP-01); 5 bags × 40 kg silica sand (for SF-INP-02 and SF-CPC-03 combined).
6.1.2  Site Preparation
  1. Confirm that concrete housekeeping pads for all three filters are complete, cured, and level (tolerance: ± 3 mm across pad surface).
  2. Confirm all pipe stubs (inlet, return, and waste/drain) are in place per approved drawing SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1 and are at correct elevations.
  3. Confirm drain connections are available and functional in the pump room.
  4. Ensure adequate lighting and ventilation in the basement pump room before commencing work.
  5. Obtain all required work permits. Conduct toolbox talk with the installation crew covering the manual handling risks, dust hazards, and the correct sand loading sequence.
6.2  Filter Tank Positioning & Anchoring
  1. Using a sack trolley and minimum 2 persons, transport each filter tank to its designated position as shown on drawing SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1.
  2. Position filter tank on the housekeeping pad. Check level using spirit level across the top flange of the filter in two perpendicular directions. Maximum allowable deviation: 3 mm/metre.
  3. Anchor filter base to the concrete pad using the provided or specified hold-down brackets. For TR140, ensure the base ring is fully in contact with the pad. For TA-40, secure the integral base feet.
  4. Verify final clearances: minimum 600 mm clear on all sides of TR140; minimum 450 mm on all sides of TA-40, for maintenance access. Verify the top of the filter has unobstructed vertical access for media loading and future valve removal.
  5. Photograph each filter in final position with the tag number visible. Record on the installation checklist.
CAUTION:All three filters (SF-ODP-01, SF-INP-02, SF-CPC-03) must be permanently fixed before sand loading begins. Do not load sand into a filter that has not been levelled and anchored.
6.3  Filter Media Loading
WARNING:This is the most critical step of the installation. Follow the sequence exactly. Wrong media type, wrong sequence, or overfilling will damage the laterals and require complete disassembly to correct.
6.3.1  TR140 (SF-ODP-01) – Gravel Support Bed Then Sand

SF-ODP-01 requires a gravel support bed underneath the sand. This is because the TR140's lateral screen openings are larger. The TA-40 (SF-INP-02, SF-CPC-03) does NOT require gravel – proceed to Section 6.3.2 for TA-40 units.

  1. Remove the multiport valve from the TR140 tank. The tank is open at the top with the center standpipe exposed.
  2. Inspect all 8 laterals at the base of the standpipe. Confirm they are fully extended and seated. Gently flex each lateral to confirm it is not brittle or cracked.
  3. [CRITICAL] Stuff a clean rag or seal the top opening of the center standpipe with tape. This prevents sand from falling into the standpipe and clogging the return line to the pool. If sand enters the standpipe, the entire tank must be disassembled and cleaned.
  4. [CRITICAL] Add clean water to fill the tank approximately one-third (1/3) full. This water cushions the impact of sand on the laterals when sand is poured. Pouring sand into a dry tank can crack or break the laterals.
  5. Add the pea gravel first (3–6 mm, approx. 4 kg / 9 lbs). Pour slowly around the standpipe, distributing evenly. This forms a 25–50 mm support bed over the laterals. Do not disturb the laterals during this step.
  6. Now add the #20 silica sand (0.45–0.55 mm, 419 kg / 925 lbs) in small batches using a clean funnel. Pour sand slowly and evenly around the standpipe – never directly onto the standpipe or laterals. After every 2 bags, check that the sand is distributing evenly.
  7. Stop loading sand when the sand level reaches the fill line marked inside the tank (approximately 2/3 to the shoulder). Do NOT fill above this line. Overfilling reduces backwash effectiveness and can cause sand to enter the multiport valve.
  8. Carefully remove the rag/tape from the standpipe. Check the standpipe opening – it must be free of sand. If sand has entered, carefully vacuum or rinse it out before proceeding.
  9. Fill the remainder of the tank with clean water to just below the top flange.
6.3.2  TA-40 (SF-INP-02 and SF-CPC-03) – Sand Only, No Gravel
  1. Remove the multiport valve. The TA-40 has a top-mounted valve seat at the center. The center tube/standpipe with integrated screen assembly should be visible.
  2. Inspect the internal screen/underdrain assembly. Confirm it is fully seated and undamaged.
  3. [CRITICAL] Plug or tape the top of the center standpipe to prevent sand entry.
  4. [CRITICAL] Fill the tank approximately 1/3 full with clean water to cushion sand impact on the internal screen.
  5. Add #20 silica sand (0.45–0.55 mm) for this unit (79 kg / 175 lbs) slowly and evenly around the standpipe using a clean funnel. No gravel is required for the TA-40.
  6. Fill to the fill line marked inside the tank. For the TA-40, this is typically at or just below the shoulder of the tank. Do NOT overfill.
  7. Remove the standpipe plug. Verify standpipe opening is clear of sand.
  8. Repeat this procedure identically for the second TA-40 unit (SF-CPC-03).
6.4  Multiport Valve Installation
6.4.1  SFV-ODP-01 – Praher V6 2" (on TR140)
  1. Before installing the valve, inspect the O-ring on the valve's base/hub. It must be clean, seated correctly in its groove, and free of cuts or flat spots. Apply a thin coat of O-ring lubricant (water-based silicone; never petroleum-based).
  2. Set the Praher V6 handle to the FILTER position.
  3. Lower the Praher V6 valve onto the TR140 filter neck, aligning the valve ports with the inlet and outlet pipe stubs.
  4. Secure the valve to the filter neck using the provided split-ring or collar clamp. Tighten evenly using alternating fasteners (cross-pattern) to ensure even compression of the O-ring seal. Do not overtighten.
  5. Verify the waste port, return port, and inlet port are oriented correctly per drawing SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1.
6.4.2  SFV-INP-02 & SFV-CPC-03 – Pentair Hi-Flow 1.5" (on TA-40 units)
  1. Inspect the O-ring on the Pentair Hi-Flow valve base. Apply water-based silicone lubricant.
  2. Set the valve handle to the FILTER position.
  3. Lower the Hi-Flow valve onto the TA-40 filter neck, engaging the valve hub with the filter tank collar.
  4. Rotate the retaining ring clockwise to lock the valve to the filter tank. Tighten until hand-tight plus 1/4 turn. Do not use excessive force on plastic retaining rings.
  5. Repeat for the second TA-40 unit (SF-CPC-03).
6.5  Piping & Connections
CAUTION:All piping must be installed per approved drawing SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1. Do not deviate from approved routing without written authorization from IHCC Senior Mechanical Engineer.
  1. Install isolation ball valve (true-union type) on the pump discharge line before connecting to the filter inlet port on the multiport valve. Ensure flow direction arrow on valve body faces toward the filter.
  2. Connect pump discharge to the IN port of the multiport valve using ASTM D1785 SCH 40 PVC pipe with solvent-cement joints. Allow 15 minutes curing time at room temperature before handling, 24 hours before pressure test.
  3. Install isolation ball valve on the RETURN port of the multiport valve (outlet to pool). Ensure flow direction is toward the pool.
  4. Connect filter RETURN port to the pool water return line per drawing.
  5. Connect the WASTE port of the multiport valve to the pump room floor drain using 1.5" PVC pipe. This line must slope to drain continuously with no low points that can trap water.
  6. Install glycerine-filled pressure gauge (0–100 PSI / 0–7 bar) at the filter inlet line, downstream of the isolation valve. The gauge must be readable from the valve operating position.
  7. Install flexible union connections at all filter-to-pipe connection points to allow future removal of the multiport valve without dismantling the pipework.
  8. Install a drain/bleed valve at the lowest point of each filter (base drain port) connected to the pump room drain.
  9. Label all pipework with flow direction arrows and service labels (e.g., "SF-ODP-01 INLET", "SF-ODP-01 RETURN"). Label all valves with valve tag number and OPEN/CLOSE direction.
  10. Check all solvent-cement joints and threaded connections visually. Allow full 24-hour curing before pressure testing.
6.6  Initial Backwash, Rinse & System Start-Up
WARNING:Before starting the pump for the first time after sand loading, the filter MUST go through a backwash and rinse cycle. This removes fine sand particles, manufacturing dust, and debris that will otherwise discharge into the pool and permanently cloud the water.
Step A – First Backwash
  1. Confirm all isolation valves are in the correct open/closed positions per the filter piping diagram.
  2. Confirm waste line valve is OPEN and connected to drain.
  3. Set multiport valve to BACKWASH position. Confirm handle is firmly detented in BACKWASH – not between positions.
  4. Start the associated circulation pump. Water will flow in reverse through the filter (bottom up), lifting and fluidising the sand bed. Gray/brown cloudy water will discharge from the waste line. This is normal.
  5. Run BACKWASH for a minimum of 5 minutes, or until the water discharging from the waste line runs clear and the sight glass (if installed) shows clear water.
  6. STOP THE PUMP. Wait 5 seconds for water flow to fully stop.
Step B – Rinse
  1. Set multiport valve to RINSE position. Confirm detent click.
  2. Start the pump. Water flows in the normal FILTER direction but discharges to waste instead of to the pool. This compacts the sand bed and clears any remaining backwash turbulence.
  3. Run RINSE for 1 to 2 minutes until the sight glass or waste discharge runs completely clear.
  4. STOP THE PUMP.
Step C – Normal Filter Operation
  1. Set multiport valve to FILTER position. Confirm detent click.
  2. Check that the waste line valve is OPEN (it remains open; the multiport valve itself routes water correctly).
  3. Start the pump. Check pressure gauge reading. Record this as the "clean filter operating pressure" (baseline pressure). This is the reference value for scheduling future backwash cycles.
  4. Inspect all connections and joints under pressure for any leaks. Mark any leaks with tape and stop the pump before repairing. Re-test after repair.
  5. Check flow rate at pool return outlets to confirm correct filtration flow.
Backwash Trigger: When the filter inlet pressure rises 7–10 PSI (0.5–0.7 bar) above the recorded clean filter baseline pressure, initiate a backwash → rinse → filter cycle. Record baseline pressure for each filter separately.

8.  TESTING & COMMISSIONING
8.1  Hydrostatic Pressure Test

Prior to commissioning, conduct a hydrostatic pressure test on all filter piping connections with the multiport valve in the FILTER position.

#Test DescriptionAcceptance CriteriaResult / Remarks
1 Isolate filters from pool and fill piping system with clean water. Pressurize to 1.5× design pressure: 75 PSI (5.2 bar) for TR140 circuit; 75 PSI (5.2 bar) for TA-40 circuits. Pressure maintained stable (< 0.2 PSI drop) for minimum 30 minutes. No visible leaks at any joint or connection.
2 Inspect all solvent-cement joints, threaded connections, valve bodies, and gauge connections under test pressure. Zero visible leaks. Any seepage from any joint is cause for test failure and repair.
3 Release test pressure, repair any defects found, and re-test from the beginning. Test must be passed in full before commissioning. Submit signed pressure test report to Consultant.
8.2  Functional / Commissioning Tests
#Test DescriptionAcceptance CriteriaResult / Remarks
1 Operate each multiport valve through all 6 positions (with pump OFF each time) and verify correct water routing at each position. Valve handle seats firmly in each position (audible click). Water routes correctly. No bypass leakage between positions.
2 Run each filter in BACKWASH for 5 minutes. Observe waste line discharge for all three units. Water at waste line runs clear within 5 minutes. Pressure during backwash within normal range.
3 Run RINSE for 2 minutes after backwash for all three filter units. Waste water completely clear by end of rinse cycle.
4 Set to FILTER and run at design flow for 30 minutes. Record inlet pressure for all three units. Inlet pressure stable, within manufacturer specification. No abnormal noise or vibration.
5 Record baseline clean filter pressure for each unit separately. Baseline pressures documented and signed: SF-ODP-01: ___ PSI  |  SF-INP-02: ___ PSI  |  SF-CPC-03: ___ PSI
6 Check pool water clarity after 24 hours of filter operation. Water visibly clear. No sand particles visible at pool return jets.
WARNING:If sand is observed at the pool return jets during commissioning, STOP the pump immediately. Sand in the return line indicates either: (1) overfilled filter tank, (2) damaged lateral, or (3) multiport valve internal damage. Do not operate until the cause is identified and corrected.
