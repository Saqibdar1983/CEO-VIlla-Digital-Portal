1.  PURPOSE & SCOPE
1.1  Purpose

This Method Statement defines the approved procedure for receiving, inspecting, installing, connecting, and commissioning all chemical water treatment equipment for the SPA & Pool Facility of the CEO Villa Project. The document ensures full compliance with the respective manufacturer installation and operation manuals, NSF/ANSI Standard 50, applicable electrical codes, and the project quality and safety plan.

1.3  Sequence of Chemical Treatment — Process Order

All equipment must be installed in the following process order in the piping return line. The sequence is critical; incorrect order causes chemical interference or equipment damage:

StepEquipment TagEquipmentRemarks
1Pump → FilterCirculation Pump & Sand FilterAlways first. Clean filtered water feeds all downstream equipment.
2UV-C 01Elecro H.R. UV-C PRO HRP-110UV must be BEFORE chemical injection. UV destroys chemicals if placed downstream.
3CF-01-IDP / CF-02-CPCHayward CL200 Chlorine FeederChlorine injection AFTER UV (UV would otherwise destroy the chlorine).
4CDP-01 / CDP-02PULSAFEEDER Dosing PumpspH adjustment chemicals injected into return line after chlorine.
5PHC Flow CellHayward HCC2000 Probe/Sensor CellFlow cell installed downstream of filters, UPSTREAM of all chemical injection to read untreated water. Control signal feeds CDP-01/CDP-02.
6→ Pool ReturnReturn Nozzles to PoolBalanced, treated water returned to the pool.
CRITICAL WARNING:The UV-C sterilizer MUST be installed upstream (before) of any chemical injection points. Placing UV downstream of chlorine dosing will destroy the chlorine and the UV lamp quartz sleeve will degrade rapidly. Confirm this sequence on the approved shop drawing before installation.
1.4  Scope of Work
  • Receiving inspection and MIR submission for all chemical treatment equipment
  • Installation of Marjan 4,000L balance tank (VT-01) on level pad
  • Installation of TankPAC TP-19P RO tank (ROT-01) in pump room
  • Installation of Elecro UV-C sterilizer (UV-C 01): pipe connections, electrical, and lamp verification
  • Installation of Hayward CL200 chlorine feeders (CF-01-IDP, CF-02-CPC): in-line piping and connections
  • Installation of PULSAFEEDER chemical dosing pumps (CDP-01, CDP-02): suction tube to chemical tank, injection into return line
  • Installation of Hayward HCC2000 pH/ORP controller (PHC): flow cell, pH sensor, ORP sensor, and connections to chemical dosing pumps
  • Electrical connections for all equipment: UV-C, CDP-01, CDP-02, PHC (all GFCI-protected circuits)
  • Initial system fill, chemical balancing, and functional commissioning test

5.  HEALTH, SAFETY & ENVIRONMENT
5.1  Chemical Hazards
WARNING:Pool chemicals – chlorine tablets, pH reducers, and ORP chemicals – are classified as hazardous substances. Mixing different pool chemicals (especially Trichlor with Calcium Hypochlorite) can cause fire, explosion, and release of toxic chlorine gas. NEVER mix chemicals. Store each chemical separately in sealed, labeled containers.
HazardRiskControl Measure
Trichlor Chlorine Tablets Inhalation of chlorine fumes – toxic at high concentrations. Skin and eye chemical burn. Fire/explosion if mixed with Calcium Hypochlorite. Do not inhale fumes. Handle with acid-resistant nitrile gloves and chemical splash goggles. Store separately from other chemicals. Emergency eyewash within 10 seconds travel.
pH Adjustment Chemicals pH reducers (dry acid) are corrosive. Skin burns, eye damage if splashed. Full PPE: acid-resistant gloves, chemical splash goggles, apron. Never add water to acid; add acid to water.
UV-C Ultraviolet Light Direct exposure to UV-C light causes severe eye damage and skin burns in seconds. UV-C is invisible – you cannot see when a lamp is lit. NEVER look at an illuminated UV-C lamp. Confirm power is OFF before opening UV-C housing. Safety interlock/flow switch prevents dry operation.
Electrical – Wet Environment Electrocution risk. All chemical equipment is near water. GFCI is mandatory. All circuits must be GFCI protected. Licensed electrician only. Test all GFCI breakers before commissioning. LOTO before any electrical maintenance.
Chemical Over-Dosing Excessive chlorine or pH chemicals in pool water causes eye/skin/respiratory irritation to bathers. HCC2000 controller monitors and limits dosing. Set alarm limits. Never allow bathers in pool until water chemistry confirmed in range by manual test kit.
Slips in Pump Room Wet floors from plumbing connections and chemical spills. Non-slip footwear. Dry spills immediately. Chemical spill kit on site.
5.2  Chemical Storage Requirements (NFPA 400)
  • Store all pool chemicals in a dedicated, locked, well-ventilated chemical storage room. Never store chemicals in the pump room where electrical equipment is present.
  • Keep Trichlor tablets completely separate from Calcium Hypochlorite (shock chlorine) and all other chemicals. Minimum 3m separation or separate locked storage required.
  • Ensure the chemical storage room has a floor drain (not connected to pool drain system) and adequate cross-ventilation to the outside.
  • Post MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheets) for all chemicals on the outside of the chemical storage room door.
  • Emergency eyewash station must be within 10 seconds travel of the chemical mixing and loading area.
5.3  PPE Requirements

All chemical handling personnel: acid-resistant nitrile gloves (minimum), chemical splash goggles (sealed), acid/chemical-resistant apron, safety shoes. When inspecting or servicing UV-C unit: confirm power is OFF before opening (UV-C light causes immediate eye/skin injury). When working on electrical: insulated gloves (CAT III), LOTO at DB.

6.  INSTALLATION PROCEDURE
6.1  Pre-Installation Activities
  1. Inspect all equipment on delivery. Check for shipping damage. Verify nameplate tags match the approved asset register: UV-C 01 (HRP-110, S/N 283234), CF-01-IDP and CF-02-CPC (Hayward CL200, S/N as listed), CDP-01 (S/N 892419) and CDP-02 (S/N 892417), PHC (HCC2000, S/N 1W23205-2256669), ROT-01 (TankPAC TP-19P).
  2. Submit MIR for all chemical treatment equipment. Obtain written consultant approval before any installation begins.
  3. Verify all approved shop drawings (SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1) are on site and that the installation sequence shown on the drawing matches Section 1.3 of this Method Statement. Any discrepancy must be raised with IHCC Sr. Mechanical Engineer before proceeding.
  4. Confirm all pool circulation pumps and sand filters are installed and tested (per MS-PUMP-SPA-01 and MS-FILTER-SPA-02) before installing chemical treatment equipment. Chemical treatment is always the LAST step in the pipework installation sequence.
  5. Confirm GFCI-protected electrical outlets are available at: UV-C 01 location (30mA RCD), PHC controller location (15A dedicated GFCI), and CDP-01 & CDP-02 locations.
6.2  Marjan 4,000L Balance Tank (VT-01)
  1. Confirm the VT-01 installation location per drawing. The base must be a completely flat, level concrete pad. The entire tank base must rest flat on the pad – do not place on beams or intermittent blocks, even if they appear level.
  2. Lift tank (with suitable equipment rated for the weight) and position on the concrete pad. Verify all four sides of the base ring contact the pad evenly.
  3. Connect the inlet water supply and tank drain/overflow per the approved piping schematic. Connections use standard poly fittings screwed into the tank fittings.
  4. Do not apply heavy load or side stress to the tank fitting connections.
6.3  RO Storage Tank (ROT-01)
  1. Position ROT-01 in the designated pump room location per drawing. The tank is pre-charged at the factory at 7 PSI bladder pressure; do not deflate.
  2. Connect the tank to the RO system feed and output lines per the RO schematic. ROT-01 is for RO system use only – do not connect to the main pool water supply line.
  3. Maximum working pressure is 100 PSI. Ensure the RO system pressure never exceeds this limit.
  4. Inspect the tank periodically. If the tank shows any sign of corrosion, remove it from service immediately.
6.4  UV-C Sterilizer Installation (UV-C 01 — Elecro H.R. UV-C PRO HRP-110)
WARNING:UV-C MUST be installed downstream of all pumps, filters, and heaters but UPSTREAM of ALL chemical injection points. Violating this sequence destroys the chlorine and causes premature lamp failure.
6.4.1  Mounting
  1. Identify the UV-C 01 installation location on the return pipe, downstream of the sand filter and upstream of the CF chlorine feeders, per drawing SB-ASB-SD-ME-008-R1.
  2. Mount the UV-C unit horizontally OR vertically on a solid wall using the fixing kit supplied. If horizontal, the 1m service clearance must be on either the left or right side. If vertical, 1m clearance must be above or below the unit.
  3. Secure all four mounting bolts firmly. The unit must not move or vibrate in operation.
WARNING:The unit MUST be in a dry, permanent weatherproof area. Water or moisture entering the enclosure voids warranty and creates electrical hazard.
6.4.2  Pipework Connection
  1. The UV-C is supplied with unions for connection to 2" (50mm) or 63mm rigid pipe. Reducers are supplied for 50mm / 1.5" pipe. Use the correct union size for the existing return pipe diameter.
  2. Apply ABS cement for ABS-to-ABS connections or Transition cement for PVC-to-ABS connections. Do NOT use PTFE tape on the UV-C union connections – the O-rings (supplied) seal the unions; apply O-rings in the groove on the manifold assembly end.
  3. The factory flow direction is: water enters LEFT side, exits RIGHT side. If the site pipe layout requires reversed flow, rotate the flow switch 180 degrees (half turn). Do not lift the flow switch paddle more than 5mm from its housing or force it.
  4. After cementing, allow full cure time before pressurising (per cement manufacturer – typically 15 minutes minimum for pressure, 24 hours for full cure).
6.4.3  Electrical Connection
  1. The UV-C 01 must be connected by a licensed electrician. Install a 30mA RCD on the supply circuit. Earth/ground the unit as required.
  2. Do NOT run the unit dry (without water flow). The flow switch included will shut the unit off if there is no flow. Confirm the flow switch is correctly installed and the paddle moves freely.
  3. Do not cover the UV-C unit in operation. The unit must have free air circulation around it.
6.5  Chlorine Feeder Installation (CF-01-IDP & CF-02-CPC — Hayward CL200)
DANGER:Never use fast-dissolving tablets. ONLY slow-dissolving 3" Trichlor tablets (99% Trichlor-s triazinetrione). NEVER install directly into copper plumbing. If brass or bronze valves are present in the system, consult a specialist before connecting chlorine feeders.
6.5.1  Installation Position
  1. CF-01-IDP (Indoor Pool): Install in the return line AFTER the heater (EWH-INP-01) or after the filter if no heater is installed. This feeder is also downstream of UV-C 01.
  2. CF-02-CPC (Cold Plunge Pool): Install in the return line AFTER the cold plunge chiller (CPC-01) and downstream of UV-C 01.
  3. NSF-50 requires a flow indication device upstream of both CL200 feeders. Confirm flow indicator (flow sight glass or flow switch) is installed before the feeder inlet.
6.5.2  Plumbing Connection
  1. Both CL200 feeders have 1.5" female NPT connections (inlet and outlet). Connect using 1.5" male union connectors. Thread connections hand tight plus 1 to 1.5 turns only – do not over-tighten. Use only Teflon tape or Hayward-approved pipe sealant formulated for ABS plastic.
  2. Install a corrosion-resistant in-line check valve at the feeder outlet to prevent backflow of chlorine gas when the system shuts off.
  3. Install a flow control valve (supplied with CL200 or installed separately) to adjust chlorine output rate.
  4. Verify the return line from the feeder back to the pool is unrestricted – never isolate the outlet line while the pump is running.
  5. After completing connections, restart the pump and check all connections for leaks. Re-tighten as required.
6.5.3  First Loading with Trichlor Tablets
  1. Before loading any chemicals, confirm the pool water has been balanced (pH 7.2–7.8, alkalinity 80–120 ppm, chlorine 1.0–1.5 ppm).
  2. With pump OFF and flow control valve set to ZERO: remove feeder cover. Load slow-dissolving 3" Trichlor tablets. Secure cover firmly.
  3. Start the pump. Set the flow control valve to the desired dosing rate per the chemical supplier's recommendation for the pool volume. Check residual chlorine daily and adjust the valve accordingly.
6.6  Chemical Dosing Pump Installation (CDP-01 & CDP-02 — PULSAFEEDER Series 100)
6.6.1  Positioning
  1. Position CDP-01 and CDP-02 close to their respective chemical storage tanks. Mount securely on the wall or on a shelf rated for the weight and chemical environment.
  2. If the injection point in the pipe is LOWER than the chemical storage tank, an anti-siphon valve must be installed in the discharge line to prevent uncontrolled siphoning of chemicals into the pool.
6.6.2  Suction and Discharge Connections
  1. Install the suction tube (polyethylene tubing) from the pump inlet into the chemical storage tank, weighting the suction end with the strainer assembly (supplied). The strainer assembly must be fully submerged in the chemical solution.
  2. Verify the arrow on the pump head and on the injection fitting both point upward. This is the correct orientation for the check valves inside the pump to work correctly. If the arrows point downward, the pump is mounted upside-down – reposition.
  3. Install the discharge tube from the pump outlet to the injection point on the return pipe. The injection fitting must be installed in a pressurised section of the return pipe. Drill and tap the injection port as required, or use an injection saddle fitting.
  4. Hand-tighten all plastic connections. Do NOT use a wrench on plastic tubing connectors.
  5. Remove all protective fitting caps from the tubing fittings before installing tubing.
6.6.3  Electrical Connection
  1. CDP-01 and CDP-02 use standard 3-prong grounded plugs (230V/1PH). Plug into GFCI-protected outlets. If hard-wiring, engage a licensed electrician.
  2. Verify the supply voltage matches the pump nameplate: 230V/1PH/60Hz. If the voltage does not match, do not energize the pump.
6.6.4  Calibration
  1. Before injecting chemicals, calibrate each pump under actual operating conditions: fill the suction tube with the actual chemical, run the pump for a measured time, collect and measure the output volume. Adjust the output dial on the pump face to achieve the required dosing rate. Record calibration data.
6.7  pH/ORP Controller Installation (PHC — Hayward HCC2000)
WARNING:The HCC2000 controller must be connected to a dedicated GFI-protected circuit. The manufacturer requires a 15A maximum breaker or fuse. NEVER use an extension cord.
6.7.1  Controller Mounting
  1. Mount the HCC2000 controller on a vertical wall at an easily accessible height (1200–1500mm from floor). Select a location: minimum 10 feet (3.0m) from the pool edge; away from corrosive chemical storage; near a GFI-protected power source; facilitating a maximum combined tubing run of 9m (30 feet) to the flow cell location.
  2. Securely mount the PVC backboard (supplied) on the wall with appropriate anchors. Fasten the controller unit to the backboard.
6.7.2  Flow Cell Installation
  1. The flow cell must be installed downstream of the sand filter but UPSTREAM of all chemical injection points (before UV-C 01, before CL200 feeders, before CDP dosing injection). This allows the controller to measure untreated water.
  2. Drill and tap a 1/4" NPT port at the influent location (downstream of filter, upstream of UV). Install a 1/4" NPT × 3/8" True-Seal tubing connector (supplied). Run 3/8" OD poly tubing to the influent flow cell port (labelled on flow cell).
  3. Drill and tap a second 1/4" NPT port at a location subject to vacuum or reduced pressure (typically on the suction side of the pump or at a lower pressure point). Install the second tubing connector. Run tubing to the effluent flow cell port.
  4. Cut a 75–150mm (3–6 inch) length of flex tubing and insert into the sample stream port on the flow cell.
6.7.3  Sensor Installation
  1. Remove pH and ORP sensors from their plastic storage bottles. Save the bottles and storage fluid for future use when sensors need to be removed for cleaning or storage.
  2. Thread the pH sensor and ORP sensor into the designated ports on the flow cell. Hand-tighten.
  3. Remove BNC connector protective covers from the left side of the HCC2000 controller (save for future transport). Connect the pH sensor cable to the pH BNC input, ORP sensor cable to the ORP BNC input, and flow sensor cable to the flow sensor input – all as labelled. Sensor cables are specialized – NEVER cut or splice.
6.7.4  Chemical Feeder Connections to Controller
  1. Connect CDP-01 power output from the HCC2000 pH Feed output (120/240VAC, 4A) to control CDP-01 operation. When the controller senses pH is out of setpoint range, it will activate CDP-01 to dose the pH adjustment chemical.
  2. Connect the ORP/chlorine feeder control from the HCC2000 Chem Feed Dry Contact (5VDC, 1A Max) to CDP-02 or the chlorine feeder control relay as required by the project controls design.
  3. Check all connections are secure and no cables are under tension or in contact with water.
6.8  System Pre-Commissioning
  1. With all equipment installed but pumps OFF: open all isolation valves on the chemical treatment system piping. Verify no valves are closed that should be open in normal operation.
  2. Start the main circulation pump (at low speed if VFD). Check all new connections for leaks: UV-C unions, CL200 connections, dosing pump injection fittings, and flow cell tubing connections. Allow 10 minutes and re-inspect. Tighten any leaking fittings.
  3. Verify the UV-C flow switch operates: with flow running, the UV-C should power on. With flow stopped (pump off), confirm UV-C shuts down via the flow switch interlock.
  4. Confirm HCC2000 display powers on and shows pH and ORP readings. Initial readings will be arbitrary until water chemistry is stable.
  5. Test the HCC2000 flow sensor: with circulation running, the controller should show "FLOW" indicated. With pump off, the controller should stop all chemical dosing automatically.
  6. Do NOT load chlorine tablets or any chemicals into feeders until water chemistry has been manually balanced and tested.
6.9  Water Chemistry Balancing & First Start-Up
CAUTION:Manual water testing with an accurate test kit is required before and during commissioning. Automated controllers (HCC2000) are an aid to maintaining chemistry, NOT a substitute for manual testing. Always take and record manual readings in accordance with health authority requirements.
  1. Before activating the chemical dosing systems, manually test and adjust the pool water chemistry to bring it within the following ranges: pH 7.4–7.6; Free Chlorine 1.0–1.5 ppm; Total Alkalinity 80–120 ppm; Calcium Hardness (pool-specific, consult water specialist); Cyanuric Acid 0–100 ppm.
  2. Power on the HCC2000 controller. Allow the pH and ORP sensors to stabilize in the water flow for a minimum of 15–30 minutes before reading values. Initial readings may drift while sensors equilibrate.
  3. Calibrate the pH sensor per the HCC2000 manual calibration procedure using the calibration buffer solutions. Record the calibration values.
  4. Set the HCC2000 pH setpoint to 7.5 (factory default) and ORP setpoint to 650 mV (factory default). Confirm these with the consultant or pool operator before activating automatic dosing mode.
  5. Activate the HCC2000 in AUTO mode. Monitor for the first 30 minutes: confirm the pH reading on the controller matches a manual test kit reading (within 0.1 pH). If they do not agree, recalibrate the pH sensor.
  6. Load Trichlor tablets into CF-01-IDP and CF-02-CPC as described in Section 6.5.3. Set the flow control valve to a low initial setting. Monitor chlorine residual daily for the first week and adjust.
  7. Do NOT allow bathers into any pool until: water chemistry is confirmed in range by both controller and manual test kit; all chemical dosing equipment has been checked for correct operation; and the IHCC Sr. Mechanical Engineer has signed off the commissioning data sheet.
